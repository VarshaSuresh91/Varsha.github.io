Teaching / Computer Science

Make ideas clear.
Make learning active.

I connect theoretical concepts with practical examples, scaffolded exercises, and regular feedback to help students develop understanding and confidence.

01 / Philosophy

An inclusive approach
to learning.

I aim to create a supportive classroom in which students can ask questions, test their understanding, and learn from mistakes. My approach uses clear explanations, transparent expectations, and multiple opportunities to practise.

02 / Teaching areas

From foundations
to application.

Course topics and teaching interests.

Software Engineering

Software development life cycles, requirements engineering, SRS quality, design principles, testing, and Agile approaches.

Software Project Management

Project planning, estimation, activity scheduling, risk management, resource allocation, and monitoring.

Programming & Data Analysis

03 / In the classroom

01

Start with a question

Use a concrete problem, short poll, or prediction exercise to surface students’ initial understanding.

02

Work through an example

Connect abstract concepts to small programs and financial software scenarios, such as loan applications and risk-rating systems.

03

Practise and reflect

Use guided tasks, quizzes, and feedback to help learners explain their reasoning and identify gaps.

04 / Example activity

Requirements
under the microscope.

Can we test this requirement?

Initial statement: “The loan application should respond quickly.”

Discussion: Which operation? How quickly? Under what workload? What result must be returned?

Learning goal: Rewrite vague requirements into precise, measurable specifications and explain how to test them.
